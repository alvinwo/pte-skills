# SGD and RTS: turn methods into practice

These are original technique drills, not real or predicted exam items. The SGD passage is shortened and is not a full-length simulation. Use separately played audio or another reader for the first listening attempt. Reading the transcript directly is organization practice, not evidence of listening performance. The coach waits for an attempt before showing analysis or a model answer.

## SGD: turn notes into a summary

Train note-taking, then oral linking, then combine them in a timed attempt with new audio: 10 seconds preparation, up to two minutes speaking. No community minimum duration is a score guarantee; do not invent detail to fill the timer.

Original short transcript — hide before the first listening attempt:

- A: I think our research group should meet online because two members commute from another city. Travelling here every week costs them both time and money.
- B: Online meetings would help them, but last time our internet connection kept dropping. We could not explain the results clearly, so I would prefer meeting in the library.
- C: Why not use online meetings for weekly progress reports and meet in the library before presentations? That would reduce travel while giving us time to discuss difficult results properly.
- A: I could support that, but we still need to ask the two commuters whether the presentation dates work for them.

Post-attempt notes:

| Speaker | View | Reason/detail | Relationship/change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Online | Two commuters lose time and money | Later conditional support for hybrid; dates need checking |
| B | Library | Previous connection failures | Recognizes convenience, but has reservations |
| C | Hybrid | Weekly online updates; in-person before presentations | Combines travel savings and discussion quality |

Do not report a final group agreement: B has not responded to the proposal and dates remain open.

Illustrative answer, not a fixed template:

> The group discussed how to arrange research meetings. The first speaker favoured meeting online to reduce travel for two members. The second acknowledged that benefit but preferred the library because connection problems had disrupted their previous discussion. The third proposed weekly online updates and in-person meetings before presentations. The first speaker supported this compromise provisionally, subject to checking the commuters’ availability, so the arrangements were not yet final.

Review speaker accuracy, relationships/changes, and unsupported conclusions. Use a different topic next time rather than memorizing this answer.

## RTS: find the essentials in preparation time

Ask: **Who is the listener? What must I do? Which details must remain? What register fits?** Respond directly to that listener.

Original prompt:

> You borrowed your classmate’s laptop and promised to return it this afternoon. Your presentation has moved to tomorrow morning, so you need the laptop until noon tomorrow. Ask whether you can keep it longer and offer an alternative if your classmate needs it today.

Notes: classmate / extend loan / tomorrow noon / ask, not announce / return today if needed.

Insufficient: “I would tell my classmate about the presentation and ask for more time.” This describes a plan instead of making the request, and omits timing and an alternative.

Illustrative response:

> Hi, thanks again for lending me your laptop. I know I promised to return it this afternoon, but my presentation has been moved to tomorrow morning. Would it be okay if I kept it until noon tomorrow? I’m sorry for the change. If you need it today, I can return it as planned and borrow one from the library instead. Please let me know what works for you.

First practise including every requirement, then record within 40 seconds. Change the listener to a teacher and adjust register without losing constraints. Next practise a refusal, apology, or request for help.

| --- | --- |
| SGD speaker confusion | Fixed A/B/C positions; return to the original row |
| SGD notes do not become speech | Turn one row into a sentence, then connect two speakers |
| SGD elaboration becomes invention | Find source evidence for every added detail |
| RTS ends after one sentence | Complete the request, stated reason, timing, and required next step |
| RTS fluent but off-task | Change listener, date, and purpose; construct a new response |
